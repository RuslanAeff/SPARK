> Thank you for the revised proposal. The comparative framing is a clear improvement over treating the three applications as separate portfolio projects, and the design now reads as a coherent piece of research rather than three case descriptions placed side by side.
>
> A few elements stand out as genuinely strong. The E0 to E4 evidence classification is a solid methodological contribution in its own right, since it forces a distinction between an AI claiming a problem was solved and actual proof that it was. Bounding the analysis around development episodes rather than every commit is a sensible way to keep the dataset manageable, and the choice of SPARK as the primary longitudinal case with the other two as comparative cases is well justified given the depth of available evidence.
>
> Before this is ready to move forward as a formal proposal, I would like you to think through four points.
>
> First, all three applications were built by you, and you would also be the one classifying your own development episodes as AI error, human correction, and so on. This is a real source of potential bias that the current draft only names as a limitation rather than addresses. It would strengthen the design considerably if you built in some form of check on this, for example a fully worked coding example for one episode with explicit criteria, applied consistently before you code the rest.
>
> Second, the term novice developer needs a clear definition early in the thesis. Building Gemini-based receipt OCR, synchronized multi-output Web Audio playback, and GPU-based local transcription is not what most readers would picture as novice work. If the argument depends on this framing, you need to state precisely what makes you a novice in this context, whether that is formal education, professional experience, or something else, since the credibility of your conclusions rests on it.
>
> Third, I would ask you to consider the realism of the scope. Twenty to thirty episodes, each assessed across twelve dimensions, across three different technology stacks, is a substantial amount of work for a single thesis. It may be worth reducing the secondary cases further, or being more selective about which episodes from SynCinema and AutoSRT genuinely add comparative value against SPARK.
>
> Fourth, the framework itself would benefit from clearer operational criteria. What separates minor rework from substantial rework, and what threshold turns an AI output into a documented error rather than an underspecified prompt. Working this out now, before you start coding episodes, will save you from having to redo earlier classifications later.
>
> I think the research question is workable and the framework is close to being systematic enough, but these four points need to be resolved first. Let me know how you would like to proceed on each.
